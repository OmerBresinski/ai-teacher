From two real lessons under v4.11 (genlab runs 20261026-061910 Y2 Great Fire Detailed and 20261026-062218 Y4 Hadrian's Wall Standard, both judged 5.5/10): the Romans "Where and when" slide was one caption sentence (big-visual, 10/13 words), the patrol slide one sentence plus one point, and no slide said who ordered the wall or where it ran; five Great Fire questions opened with "Imagine"; the retrieval quiz asked "Which city needs a memorial to this fire?"; a hinge offered "Narrow streets made sparks cold". The prompt's own picture example called the 1666 Pudding Lane bakery a "named" real site, so a reconstruction went down the real-only route (ruling 210 allows AI pictures for reconstructions).

system-KS*.template.txt = the v4.11 templates with five lines changed:
- Flow (1.): a teaching slide's own words state its point's core facts for the age, each a concrete detail (name, date or place, number or size, real example); a general sentence a pupil could write without the lesson is not yet teaching; a reason, cause or effect says who or what it involves; the words teach the point when the picture fails; each sentence adds a fact (no padding).
- Layout (2.): the size counts the facts the idea states; a caption-only picture or diagram slide teaches a point only after an earlier slide has stated its facts.
- Questions: a recall question asks plainly, never inventing a reason to ask; every wrong option, in a hinge or any question with options, is an answer a pupil who missed or mixed up part of the lesson would give, never silly or impossible. The hinge belief rule is unchanged.
- Worked example: an invented case is stated plainly; imagine, suppose or pretend opens at most one question a lesson; no opening other than a question word starts more than two questions; well-known facts for the age are stated, only an uncertain figure left out. The quoted "imagine" / "suppose" examples are gone.
- Picture: "named" is a real thing as it is or as it was recorded (a place or monument as it stands, a picture made at the time of an event); a scene or reconstruction, even at a real place, is "generic" and its shows calls it a reconstruction (rulings 210, 296).
