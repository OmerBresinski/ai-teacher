## 5. The lesson-quality programme

### 5.1 Goal and targets

Set by Greg on 1 Oct, budget $8 of new spend (later $9):

- **Visual against Chalkie:** beat them on clarity, polish and lesson on slides; pictures within 0.2; win at least 2 of 3 briefs.
- **Text against the Opus oracle:** at or above Opus on at least 3 of the 7 J5-B dimensions, with no more J6 faults.

Method: iterate on small slices (3 visual + 3 text briefs), widen to the full set (9 visual, 8 text) only when a slice hits the targets, end each round with a checkpoint, and stop after two rounds with no progress.

### 5.2 Judging protocol

Defined in [PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md) and [judge-rubric-v2.md](judge-rubric-v2.md). All judging ran on in-session agents, so it cost nothing; only generation was paid.

- **J6** (primary): a yes/no fault checklist per deck: false or misleading statements, worked-example errors, a wrong key, a second defensible answer, tested before taught.
- **J5-B** (secondary): 1 to 5 on seven dimensions (pitch, coverage, explanation, practice, question quality, lesson flow, coherence); overall is their mean. Compare only within one batch.
- **J5-C** (ship gate): blind pairwise in both orders. A win counts only when both orders agree.
- **Blind visual judge against Chalkie:** decks rendered at 1440 ([lab/visual](lab/visual)), two blind passes per brief scoring clarity, pictures, polish and lesson on slides, plus a preferred deck. The Chalkie reference slides are not in the repo.
- **Slice first:** about 3+3 briefs per round; the full set only when promising.

### 5.4 What worked and why

- **Slot capacity and text size** (ruling 140, C2, base K): body at 0.7 of the display size let a teaching slide hold a whole idea. Lesson on slides went from 2.83 to above Chalkie.
- **Chunked text** (D1, E1): labelled points instead of paragraphs, with line budgets per chunk. Fixed the clarity loss from walls of prose.
- **Full worked examples** (C2, E1): reasoned steps, kept in every deck including short ones.
- **The render fix** (D, E): the harness had captured worked examples on their first build step, so judges saw blank slides. Rendering final states (and questions unanswered) removed the confound.
- **Code diagram templates** (I1): six hand-built kinds. Pictures went from -1.08 to +0.25 against Chalkie, and the particle and hydrograph diagrams finally appeared.
- **Caption and fact gates** (G1, J1): captions checked against the photo's source text, then caption claims checked against Commons place data. Wrong captions went to 0.
- **Retrieval warm-up in code** (H1): flow +0.50.
- **Model-written exit ticket** (Q): the first round where we won more J5-C pairs than Opus.
- **Latency fix** (S): the exit-items call no longer blocks the editable save, and the reasoned-step bracket rule is back on the wire schema, which removed serial writer re-asks. Editable went from 103 s (R) to 71 s.

### 5.5 What didn't work

- **Oak packs on Sol** (K): no quality gain, pitch fell where Oak's lesson was for a different year (Y3 rivers for a Y5 brief), cost up 7 to 52%.
- **D2 per-part checks by prompt:** the model placed them in 2 of 3 decks; flow did not move. (F1 then placed checks in code.)
- **M's extra check before practice:** coverage fell 1.33.
- **N's year-band table:** no pitch change.
- **luna as the writer** (L): explanation 1.00 and question quality 0.75 below Sol. Cheap and fast, with Oak it had 0 faults, but not good enough alone.
- **Earlier dead ends** (in the 30 Sep handoff): lesson designer arm F, r6 designer.

### 5.6 Measurement caveats

- **Judge noise is about 0.3 to 0.5.** The same Opus decks scored 3.50 and 3.93 overall in rounds S and S2, and 3.67 to 3.99 across rounds. Visual judges moved Chalkie's own scores by up to 0.5 between packets. Treat dimension gaps under about 0.3 as ties.
- **Mostly single runs.** Two-run averages exist only for C, E, F, G, H and I. Generation run-to-run spread reached 0.5 to 0.67 on question quality and coherence.
- **Blinding was weak.** One agent ran J6, J5-B and J5-C per order file, and judges often recognised Opus decks or had seen earlier keys.
- **Lesson-on-slides confound** (checked after J): is our win just more text or fewer pictures? Mostly not. Words per teaching slide are identical (51.6 v 51.7). Ours has about one more teaching slide and 20% more teaching words, and the template always yields a starter, worked examples, a hinge and practice, which the rubric's "tasks and questions" rewards. Judges' reasons cite structure. A fairer check (picture-blind re-judge, per-objective teachability) is unrun.
- **Overfitting:** there is no separate audit file. Round J tested slice against new briefs: visual results generalised (new briefs won 5 of 6), text stayed at 2 of 7 on both. In code, many fixes are commented with the brief that exposed them (y5 rivers, y6 ratio, y9 Weimar, Caersws), and two fixture files replay recorded y5 and y6 lessons. The rules themselves are general, but read those as regression cases, not as a sign the code is tuned to them.
