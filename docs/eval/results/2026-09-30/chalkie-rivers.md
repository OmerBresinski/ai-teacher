**B, slide 8, false (web):** "Over 200 mm of rain fell in just four hours on Bodmin Moor".
About 200 mm was the **24-hour** total at Otterham (185 mm at Lesnewth over about 5 hours), on the
high ground just inland of Boscastle, not Bodmin Moor proper. Sources: Wikipedia "2004 Boscastle
flood"; RMetS MetLink case study; Met Office 20-year blog.

Near misses, recorded but not counted ("unsure = no"):
- B s8 "two-metre wall of water": most sources give a 3 m wave after a debris-dammed bridge gave way.
- B s8 "£10 million scheme widened the channel and raised car park levels": the Environment Agency
  defence scheme was about £4.6 m; £10 m is quoted for the whole defence plus regeneration package.
  The works described (channel widened and lowered, car park raised) are correct.
- B s8 "75 cars": figures range from 50 to about 115 across sources; 75 is widely used in GCSE material.
- B s2 "over a month's worth of rainfall in under 24 hours" (Yorkshire, Nov 2019): Sheffield 84 mm
  in 24 h against a November average of about 80 mm. Holds. A s2 says the same for northern England; holds.
- Both decks list clay as an impermeable "rock" beside granite. This is standard school usage, so it
  is not flagged for either deck (the 15 Sept audit noted it as a defect).
- B s4 "rush into channels in minutes rather than hours": hyperbolic, but not flagged.
- A s12 "increases downstream flood velocity": defensible for straightening and embankments.

## Unblinded (key: A = ORIGINAL 15 Sept, B = NEW 30 Sept)

**The new deck is not better. It is slightly worse on the standard protocol:** faults 1 against 0,
objectives met 0 of 3 against 1 of 3, pairwise to the original in both orders, and 1–5 mean 3.00
against 3.43.

Against the 15 Sept audit row (the 15 Sept quality-vs-Chalkie audit, lab notes not in the repo: Chalkie Y8 rivers, overall 3.4, 1 of 3
met (1 partly, 1 not)): today's blind re-judge of the original reproduces it (3.43; 1 of 3 met with
the same split). The scales differ (the audit used 4 columns, J5-B uses 7 dimensions), so the match
is indicative rather than exact.

Three biggest differences:
1. **Objectives and practice.** Chalkie rewrote its own objectives. It dropped the hydrograph and
   changed "evaluate" into listening comprehension. The pupil's own evaluation task and its model
   answer are gone, replaced by one recognition MCQ, so no objective is fully met.
2. **Case study, with a factual error.** The new deck adds a named Boscastle case study (a gain in
   specificity), but its headline figure is wrong (200 mm was the 24-hour total, not 4 hours) and two
   other figures are shaky (2 m wave, £10 m).
3. **Pictures.** The theme is cleaner and more age-appropriate, and the scraped video thumbnail is gone.
   But the new deck ships a watermarked stock photo on the title slide and a likely AI-generated,
   non-UK flood scene on the hook slide.
